Act 1
The needle in a haystack — a fragmented market reality
1:00 – 5:00  ·  ~4 min
Slide 3
Where Commercial Ambition Meets Operational Reality
Goal: Show how IoT and multiple settlement mechanisms made the commercial side unmanageable
  • IoT growth turned one agreement per partner into 4–5 agreements
    • Roaming IoT connections projected to grow 300%, revenues from $13.8B (2023) to $23.4B by 2028.
    • 38% of enterprises need IoT across multiple continents — traditional roaming models don't fit.
  • Three settlement mechanisms now coexist: TAP, IoT Discount, BCE — often on separate billing platforms
    • TAP (Transferred Account Procedure): The industry standard since 1991. Files exchanged monthly, per-event rating, linear charging only. Built for voice — cannot handle IoT micro-sessions, threshold-based pricing, or 5G complexity.
    • BCE (Billing & Charging Evolution): GSMA-standardised successor. Aggregated reports, flexible settlement windows (monthly/quarterly/annual/custom), multi-dimensional charging models. Purpose-built for IoT and 5G.
  • Calculations handled manually → disputes, delayed settlements, slower cash flow
    • Pricing disputes alone cost $2M per MNO, taking up to 3 months to resolve.
Sources: Kaleido Intelligence · IoT Now · Syniverse – TAP vs BCE · GSMA BCE · The Fast Mode · TOMIA – 5 Roaming Challenges
Complexity has outpaced the tools operators currently use.
Slide 4
The Right Side: Network Complexity
Goal: Mirror the commercial problem on the engineering/network side
  • Every commercial segment requires its own network configuration; device types, access tech, and usage patterns all diverge
  • Ongoing 2G/3G phase-out forces continuous, dynamic steering
    • 278 completed, planned, or in-progress 2G/3G switch-offs across 83 countries — 2025 is the peak year (37 2G + 39 3G).
    • Ofcom has mapped predicted "2G Inbound Roaming Not-Spots" where legacy roaming will simply stop working.
  • Clear financial link: higher network quality → higher data consumption → more revenue
    • 5G users consume up to 2.7× more mobile data than 4G users in leading markets.
    • EU tests: 25%+ of roaming instances deliver suboptimal quality even when better was technically possible.
Sources: GSA – 2G/3G Switch-Off 2025 · GSMA Intelligence – Network Sunsets · Ofcom · Opensignal · European Commission – Roaming Performance Study
Steering is not just a technical function — it's a financial lever. If done manually, it is like finding a needle in a haystack.
Slide 5
A Fragmented Market Reality
Goal: Show the industry-wide gap — managed in isolation, partial automation, call for simplification
  • Commercial agreements, settlement, and steering managed in complete silos — some operators still on Excel
  • $2–2.3 billion in annual revenue leakage industry-wide from billing errors and operational inefficiencies
  • 60–70% of margin-impacting roaming events occur in the first 30 seconds of network attach — no human can react in time
  • Operators lose 20–30% of potential wholesale roaming margin because the revenue side stays manual while the network side is already automated
Sources: Juniper Research – $2B Revenue Leakage · Bankai Infotech
The problem isn't complexity — it's that the two sides have never talked to each other.

Slide 7
One Steering in Practice: Three Scenarios
Goal: Make the solution concrete with real-world examples
  • Example 1 — Group commitment: A group-level commitment spans multiple country affiliates. If one affiliate is miscalculated → penalty. One Steering distributes targets correctly across all.
  • Example 2 — Retail + IoT cross-commitment: Commercial team negotiates a joint retail/IoT commitment. Complex accruals and steering — handled automatically.
  • Example 3 — City-level steering (US): Network A preferred nationwide. IPN detects Network B delivers better coverage in Washington, DC → auto-steers subscribers there without breaking the commercial commitment.
Works at scale, with granularity, in real-world messy environments.
